How to Create a Professional Quotation in 5 Minutes

January 15, 2025 • 5 min read

Creating a professional quotation doesn't have to take hours. Whether you're a freelancer, contractor, or small business owner, a well-structured quotation is essential for winning clients and getting paid on time.

In this guide, you'll learn exactly how to create quotations that look professional, communicate clearly, and help close deals faster.

What is a Quotation?

A quotation (also called a quote or estimate) is a formal document that outlines:

  • What products or services you'll provide
  • How much you'll charge
  • When payment is due
  • Terms and conditions of the offer

Unlike an invoice, a quotation is sent before work begins. It's essentially a price proposal that helps clients make purchasing decisions.

Why Professional Quotations Matter

A professional quotation:

  • Builds trust with potential clients
  • Reduces back-and-forth communication
  • Sets clear expectations upfront
  • Protects you from scope creep
  • Makes you look more credible than competitors

5 Steps to Create a Professional Quotation

Example of a professional quotation template

Step 1: Add Your Business Information

Every quotation should include:

  • Your company name and logo
  • Contact information (email, phone, address)
  • Business registration number (if applicable)
  • Quotation number and date

Step 2: Include Client Details

Make it personal by adding:

  • Client name and company
  • Project name or reference
  • Client's contact information

Step 3: List Products or Services

This is the core of your quotation. Create a clear table with:

  • Description - What you're providing
  • Quantity - How many units
  • Unit Price - Price per item
  • Total Price - Quantity × Unit Price

Be specific! Instead of "Design Services", write "Logo Design - 3 Concepts with 2 Revision Rounds".

Step 4: Add Terms & Conditions

Protect yourself by including:

  • Validity period (e.g., "This quote is valid for 14 days")
  • Payment terms (e.g., "50% upfront, 50% upon completion")
  • Delivery timeline
  • What happens if scope changes
  • Cancellation policy

Step 5: Calculate Total and Add Payment Instructions

  • Subtotal of all items
  • Taxes (if applicable)
  • Grand Total
  • Payment methods accepted
  • Bank details or payment links

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  1. Vague descriptions - "Marketing services" is too broad. Be specific.
  2. No validity period - Clients may accept your quote months later when your prices have changed.
  3. Missing payment terms - When is payment due? What methods do you accept?
  4. Unclear pricing - Is tax included? Are there additional fees?
  5. Unprofessional formatting - A messy Word doc doesn't inspire confidence.

Quotation vs Invoice: What's the Difference?

Aspect Quotation Invoice
When sent Before work starts After work is done
Purpose Price proposal Payment request
Legally binding No (until accepted) Yes
